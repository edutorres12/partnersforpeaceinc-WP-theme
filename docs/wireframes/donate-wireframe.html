1. HeaderSame as all pages. Donate is not in the primary nav — it is reached from the footer and from every closing CTA, so the header keeps its single Church Partnerships action.
2. Hero + give blockThe reference page opens on three screens of prose and puts the first amount button below the fold. Here the ask IS the hero: the widget sits beside the headline so a donor who arrived ready to give never scrolls to find the form.

Monthly is preselected and $100 carries a “most common” tag — both are anchors, and both are the single highest-leverage thing on a donation page. Custom amount is a field, not a fifth tier, so no one has to hunt for it. Trust microcopy sits under the button where the hesitation actually happens, not in fine print at the bottom.
3. Impact stripFour numbers, thin band. Proof directly under the ask, for the donor who wants evidence before committing. Deliberately not a section: it is a reassurance beat between the form and the explanation.
4. What your gift doesThe reference’s amount tiers, kept — concrete impact per amount is the strongest pattern on a donation page. Rebuilt as one four-track grid so the cards share a height, same as the Services and roster grids.

Each tier repeats its own action: a donor who scrolled past the hero widget is now convinced, and should not have to scroll back up. The line underneath catches everyone the four amounts do not fit.
5. Where your gift goesImage beside the copy. Answers “what is actually funded” — the second objection after “how much”. Four priorities as headed paragraphs rather than cards: it is a list to read, not a grid to scan.
6. Monthly givingIts own dark band with its own smaller widget. Recurring donors are worth several times a one-off gift, and the ask is different enough that folding it into the hero would blunt both. “Cancel anytime” sits under the button because that is the objection that stops a recurring commitment.
7. Other ways to giveMail, employer matching, stock and legacy. Every donor who cannot or will not use a card leaves the page without this, and these are the largest gifts.
8. Giving questions + fine printThe last objections, in the order they get raised, using the site’s existing FAQ accordion. The 501(c)(3) and EIN notice sits under it — required, and it belongs where a donor goes looking for it rather than floating mid-page.
9. ScriptureOne quiet band. Sets the motive after the transactional sections rather than before them, where it would delay the ask.
10. Closing CTASame closing banner as the rest of the site, with Donate as its action.
11. FooterSame as all pages.
