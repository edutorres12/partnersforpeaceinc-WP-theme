1. HeaderSame as all pages. Donate stays out of the primary nav: it is reached from the footer and from every closing CTA, and the header already carries one action.
2. Hero + give blockThe ask IS the hero. The reference page opens on three screens of prose and puts its first amount button below the fold; here the widget sits beside the headline, so a donor who arrived ready to give never scrolls to find the form.

Three decisions, all of them anchors: $100 tagged “most common”, custom amount as a field rather than a fifth tier, trust microcopy under the button where the hesitation actually happens.

No frequency toggle. An earlier draft preselected Monthly, which is normally the highest-leverage control on a donation page. It came out because giving runs on PayPal hosted buttons and the organisation has no recurring buttons: frequency is decided on PayPal’s checkout, not here, so a toggle would be advertising a state this page cannot set. Add it the day subscription buttons exist — not before.

The impact line is the whole point. It is bound to the selection — pick $250 and it says what $250 does. That is where per-amount framing belongs. A separate grid of amount cards further down says the same thing twice and splits the action across two places.

Not the hero block. wptpl/hero with a background image renders full-bleed with a single column of text and cannot hold a card beside it. This band is the Contact page’s form-beside-card pattern instead — see the block mapping at the bottom of this column. The three trust lines are wptpl/checklist, not hand-written rows.
3. Where your gift goesThe one persuasion section that survives. Answers “what is actually funded”, which is the objection that follows “how much”. Three priorities, not four: the fourth was a variation on the third, and a list that runs past three stops being read.

Image beside the copy, so the page has one photographic beat between the widget and the fine print.
4. Giving questionsFour, using the site’s existing FAQ accordion. Tax-deductibility, destination, receipts, payment security — the four that stop a donation. Receipts earn a slot specifically because PayPal sends its own confirmation, which is not the acknowledgement a donor needs at tax time; saying so here prevents the email that otherwise follows every gift.
5. Other ways to give + fine printMail, employer matching, stock and legacy. Deliberately compact — no cards, no icons. These routes carry the largest gifts but the smallest traffic: they need to be findable, not prominent, and three big cards gave them the same weight as the ask itself.

The 501(c)(3) and EIN notice closes the band. Required, and this is where a donor goes looking for it.
6. Closing — scripture + CTAOne band, not two. The scripture and the closing banner were both quiet centred bands doing the same job back to back; merging them keeps the motive and the action in one beat. Comes after the transactional sections, never before — ahead of the widget it only delays the ask.
7. FooterSame as all pages.
Block mappingEvery band below is built from blocks that already exist. Nothing here needs a new one except the widget internals.

1. header.php
2. wptpl_cover + wptpl_columns holding [copy | a wptpl-form group], the same shape Contact uses for its form beside the practice-info card. Trust lines: wptpl/checklist.
3. wptpl_columns + wptpl_image + wptpl/section-header — the image-beside-copy split from About.
4. wptpl/faq, exact fit, one items array.
5. wptpl/feature-card set transparent and unbordered, in a wptpl-card-grid; fine print is a wptpl_paragraph.
6. wptpl/cta-banner, theme: dark — quote in headline, attribution in eyebrow.
7. footer.php

The one new surface is the widget: amount tiles, custom-amount field, impact line. Nothing in the theme draws those, and like Contact’s form it is not the seeder’s job — the seeder writes a marked placeholder inside the wptpl-form wrapper the theme already styles, and the real markup replaces it.

Giving runs on PayPal hosted buttons, one per amount, which the site already has:

Four tiles plus the custom-amount field map onto those five ids. The tiles do not need a payment SDK: a form posting to paypal.com/donate with the chosen id in a hidden input keeps the widget looking exactly like this, because the visible control is your own submit button rather than PayPal’s. Verify the ids and their current settings in the PayPal account before wiring — they were read off the existing page, not from the account.

The impact line is site-side text, swapped on selection before the handoff, so it works regardless.

Porting note: this wireframe draws cards at 14px radius and 20px gaps, the house style of the other wireframes. The shipped values come from the CSS tokens: --wptpl-card-radius (20px), --wptpl-gap-cards (1.5rem). Do not type the wireframe’s numbers into the seeder.
Cut from the first draftFour bands were removed. Recording why, so they do not get re-added:

Impact stat strip. Four metrics a practice this size has not measured. Invented numbers on a donation page are a trust risk, not proof.

“What your gift does” tier grid. The same four amounts as the widget, with buttons to the same destination. Folded into the widget’s impact line.

Monthly giving band. A second ask for what the widget’s toggle offered. The toggle itself then went too, once giving turned out to run on one-time PayPal buttons — see band 2. Recurring giving is the biggest single lift available to this page, and it is currently unavailable at any position in the layout. That is a PayPal account question, not a design one.

Standalone scripture band. Merged into the closing CTA.

Net: 11 bands to 7, one donation action instead of nine, and roughly 40% less scroll (4,235px to 2,637px at 1400px wide).
