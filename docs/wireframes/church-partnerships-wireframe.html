1. HeaderSame as all pages. Church Partnerships is the header CTA, so this page is the one the button lands on — it has to answer “what is this” before it asks for anything.
2. HeroThe reference page has no hero at all: it opens on “How It Works”, so a pastor arriving from the nav reads three steps for a programme nobody has named yet, then four prices. Everything the page is selling lives in the visitor’s head or nowhere.

This band states what a partnership is, for whom, and what it costs to start — then splits the two audiences: book a call for the leader who needs a conversation, see plans for the one who already knows and wants the number. The microcopy under the buttons kills the two objections that stop a first click: commitment and minimum size.
3. What your congregation getsThe persuasion band the reference is missing entirely. A subscription that bills monthly needs a reason before it needs a price, and “support mental health in your community” buried in step one is not it.

Three benefits, not four or six: written for the person who signs, not the person who attends. Image beside the copy, the About page split, so the page has one photographic beat before the pricing.
4. PlansThe transaction, and the page’s one heavy band: tiers, what every tier includes, how you start, and the way out if none of them fit. Four things, but all four are the same decision, so they belong in one place rather than spread across three bands with headings between them.

The reference renders the pricing three times: a heading table hidden on every breakpoint, a card row hidden on every breakpoint, and one live row — with prices that disagree ($1,300/$2,600/$6,500 hidden, $1,320/$2,640/$4,400/$6,600 live). The dead markup goes; the live numbers stay.

Ascending order, left to right. The reference’s live row runs Tier 4 first. A price ladder read backwards makes the entry point the hardest thing to find.

One anchor. Tier 2 is lifted and tagged, the same job the “most common” badge does on the Donate widget. Four undifferentiated cards make the visitor do the ranking themselves.

Per-session rate on every card. Every tier is $110 a session; showing it turns four intimidating monthly figures into one honest scale, and it is the strongest fairness signal the pricing has. Confirm the numbers against the live PayPal buttons before porting — the hidden table disagrees with them.

Inclusions once, not four times. The tiers differ only in volume, so a feature list repeated in each card is the same sentence printed four times.

Getting started is a strip, not a band. Covenant, plan, promote — the reference’s three steps, kept in full but demoted. They describe a process nobody deliberates over, and as their own section “choose a subscription” had to appear before the prices it refers to. Below the tiers it answers the question that actually follows a price: how do we start?

The custom-plan line is the reference’s asterisked footnote, promoted to a row with an action. Deliberately not a fifth card: a quote is a conversation, not a price.
5. Partnership questionsFour, using the site’s existing FAQ accordion. Eligibility, unused sessions, confidentiality and cancelling — what a church board asks before it approves a recurring line item, and none of which the reference answers anywhere.

Confidentiality earns its place: a congregant will not use this if leadership sees the attendance list, and the church cannot promise what it has not been told.

A fifth, “who provides the counselling”, was cut — Meet the Team answers it better and is one click away in the nav.
6. Closing — scripture + CTASame shape as the Donate page’s close, for the same reason: motive and action in one beat, after the transactional bands rather than before them. The CTA repeats the hero’s primary action, not a third new one.
7. FooterSame as all pages.
Block mappingEvery band is built from blocks that already exist. Nothing here needs a new one.

1. header.php
2. wptpl/hero with backgroundImageUrl, overlayOpacity: 0.6, className: wptpl-hero-dark and alignment: center — the conversion-page hero the seeder already builds. Second button is secondaryCtaText; the trust line is microcopy.
3. wptpl_columns + wptpl/section-header + wptpl_image — the image-beside-copy split from About.
4. Four wptpl/feature-card in a wptpl-card-grid, centered, each with ctaText/ctaUrl to its PayPal link; the anchored tier is the same block with eyebrow carrying the badge. Inclusions: one wptpl/checklist, columns: 4. Getting started: wptpl/steps with usePlaceholder: false and no heading, so it renders as a bare numbered row inside the band instead of its own photo section. Custom row: wptpl/cta-banner, light theme.
5. wptpl/faq, one items array.
6. wptpl/cta-banner, theme: dark — quote in headline, attribution in eyebrow.
7. footer.php

Seeder note: the page currently renders from wptpl_seed_page_conversion(), the generic hero/body/three-cards/steps shape it shares with Payment and Donate. That shape has no pricing row, so porting this means giving Church Partnerships its own builder in scripts/seed/pages.php — the same move the Donate page needs.

Porting note: this wireframe draws cards at 14px radius and 20px gaps, the house style of the other wireframes. The shipped values come from the CSS tokens: --wptpl-card-radius (20px), --wptpl-gap-cards (1.5rem). Do not type the wireframe’s numbers into the seeder.
Cut, and whyRecording what was dropped, so it does not get re-added.

“How it works” as its own band. Five content bands, not six. Three steps with a background image and three bordered cards is a full screen of scroll spent on sign, pick, announce — and it forced “choose a subscription” to appear above the prices it points at. Same three steps, same words, one strip inside the Plans band.

The hidden tier table. Three rows of headings (“Tiers / Monthly cost / Number of clients”) marked hidden on desktop, tablet and mobile — markup that ships to every visitor and renders for none. Its numbers also contradict the live cards.

The duplicate card row. A second set of Tier 1–3 cards, also hidden everywhere, with href="#" buttons. Two rows of pricing in the DOM is how the two price lists got out of sync.

“Number of clients” as the unit. Replaced with sessions. The live cards already say sessions; the hidden table says clients. They are not the same thing, and a church buying twelve of one and receiving twelve of the other is a support ticket at best.

Per-card feature lists. Never present in the reference, and deliberately not added: with tiers this uniform they would be four copies of one list.

A partner-church testimonial. The one addition that would earn a band on a page like this, and the one we cannot fake. Add it when a real partner will say something on the record — between the value band and the plans.
