2. HeroPhoto band with a dark wash, like the Services hero. Eyebrow, H1, one sentence on how the practice picks its clinicians, one CTA. Nothing sits between this and the roster: the faces are why the page exists, and anything above them delays the payoff.
3. Counseling teamThe page's core. Six cards in ONE grid (not two rows of three) so every card shares a height — same shape as the Services grid, for the same reason.

Each card: portrait, credential line as the eyebrow ABOVE the name, a bio short enough to scan, specialties as pills, and its own action. The pills let a visitor scan by need ("who treats this?") rather than only by name. Left-aligned, not centered: centered multi-line copy turns a roster into a wall.
4. Clinical directorPortrait beside the copy, on the dark band. Two card grids stacked read as one long list — an editorial row between them breaks the rhythm and answers the second question a visitor has: who runs this practice. Links out to About rather than repeating it.
5. Leadership & supportDeliberately lighter than the roster above: role, name, one line, no portrait, no CTA. Giving an office manager the same visual weight as a therapist someone is about to trust with their marriage flattens the hierarchy the page is built on. White cards on the tinted band so they still read as cards.
6. How a match is madeSix faces is a choice, and a choice with no stated process is where a visitor stalls. Three steps + CTA + microcopy — the answer to "I don't know which one to pick". Placed after the faces, not before: it only becomes a question once the roster has been seen.
7. Join our teamA team page is read by clinicians looking for work as well as by clients looking for care, and the closing CTA below speaks only to the second. Its own photo band so the two invitations are not two banners in a row.
8. Closing CTASame closing banner as the rest of the site.
9. FooterSame as all pages.
